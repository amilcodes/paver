Why the first demo's percentage differs
The earlier ~98% learned-matcher trial was exploratory on the original 1600-pixel development view. This comparison uses a 2200-pixel view, padded masks, conservative annotated ground and common audits. The marked-lane result here is compared against SIFT under exactly those same conditions. Its percentage should not be compared directly with the original 56% SIFT demo.


What can we actually verify?
We hide road pixels whose original values are known, zero them before feature extraction, and register again. The small probes were added after seeing the large-patch abstentions; they are exploratory diagnostics, not independent evidence that a full car can be removed.

On these small probes, learned matching does not beat SIFT in aggregate coverage or error. Their accepted pixel sets differ; only each row's Telea comparison uses identical pixels. All corroborated local-probe pixels come from Bordeaux, so the strict policy's zero errors above 20 among 4,087 pixels should not be generalized.
What the evidence supports
  • Learned matching increases candidate transfers on several streets under common geometric and appearance checks.
  • Per-pixel donor identity, donor count and disagreement are saved at full resolution.
  • Stricter agreement policies produce a much smaller proposed repair; they have not been calibrated as probabilities.
What still blocks reliable full-car recovery
  • Texture-poor asphalt and repeated paving can leave too few reliable anchors.
  • Large hidden patches remove much of the evidence needed to support a planar warp.
  • Shadows, raised objects, camera seams and departures from a flat road can survive a global homography check.
MAE uses RGB values on a 0–255 scale. The >20 threshold is a descriptive photometric error proxy, not a geometric wrong-transfer probability. Pooled pixels are spatially correlated and scenes are not a random sample. No confidence interval or universal “reliability rate” is claimed.
